## TL;DR

Every campaign carries a product or two that spends without producing. The reflex is pause-or-ignore; both skip the diagnosis. Six issue tags map "no output" to six different causes — and each cause wants a different action.

## You saw "no output" but never asked "from what stage"

Every campaign's product list has them: spend climbing, output absent. The standard responses — kill it, or look away — share one flaw: neither asks **where** the product fails.

"No output" is six different diseases. Tagging is how "this feels dead" becomes "it dies at step three, here's the fix."

## The six tags

**Tag 1: GMV sliding.** Orders decline across consecutive settled periods with no floor in sight. Product or market fit is decaying — check the listing and price competitiveness first; ad-side tweaks are secondary.

**Tag 2: Cost up, GMV down.** The worst shape: both ends worsening together. Competition is repricing your traffic while your page converts less of it — the product's value story is failing at market price.

**Tag 3: Zero add-to-cart.** A full window, not a single cart. People look and nobody stays — the listing or price loses them at the first gate. Scaling spend on this product just scales the walk-away.

**Tag 4: High bounce.** They arrive and leave, week after week. Traffic arrives; the first screen gives them no reason to stay: price, main image, headline promise.

**Tag 5: Buried impressions.** Same campaign, siblings get impressions, this product gets almost none. The platform isn't surfacing it — typically a competitiveness verdict (price band, click-through, relevance) already made upstream. No bidding strategy rescues it; fix the product's competitive basics.

**Tag 6: Dual-low conversion.** Both quote-conversion and lead-conversion run below the campaign's normal level simultaneously. Visitors engage but stall at shallow depth — an internal quality issue: listing persuasion, quote responsiveness, sales follow-up.

## How to use the tags

| Outcome | Action |
|---------|--------|
| One tag | Observe; fix the named funnel stage |
| Two or more stacked | Removal candidate; review with priority |
| No tags, flat performance | Hold; normal variation |

Two discipline notes: judge on **settled periods only** — unsettled data reverses itself (the settlement mechanics are in [Why marketplace ad data needs 16 days before you judge it](/blog/1688-p4p-ad-data-16-day-settlement)); and one tag is not a death sentence — single-tag hits are often weather, stacked tags are climate.

<InfoBox variant="warning" title="One sentence to remember">

Tag first, act second: six tags, six causes. One tag gets observation and a fix; stacked tags get the removal review. Removal is the last step of diagnosis, not a substitute for it.

</InfoBox>

## FAQ

### How do I diagnose a marketplace product that burns ad spend with no output?

Tag it before touching it: six issue labels — consecutive GMV decline, cost-up-GMV-down, zero add-to-cart, high bounce, bottom-tier impressions, dual-low conversion — each points to a different cause and fix.

### Does one issue tag justify removing a product from ads?

No. One tag means observe and fix that specific funnel stage. Two or more stacked tags signal systematic failure — that is the removal candidate.

### What does bottom-tier impression share mean?

Within the same campaign, siblings get impressions while this product gets almost none. The platform isn't surfacing it — that's a product-competitiveness problem, not a bidding problem.
