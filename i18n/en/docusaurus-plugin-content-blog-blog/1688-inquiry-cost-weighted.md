## TL;DR

"Spend ÷ inquiries" treats a purchase-ready quote request and a idle browser click the same. Weighted inquiry cost — grade, weight, then divide — shows what your ads actually buy, and the target line comes from your own history, not the industry's.

## Two campaigns, same cost, completely different business

Campaign A and Campaign B both run at $4 per inquiry. The report calls it a tie.

Open the conversations: half of A's inquiries ask for volume pricing and samples; half of B's ask questions answered by the product page. Same number, different animals.

Raw cost-per-inquiry flattens quality into quantity. It isn't useless — it's too coarse: coarse enough to hide problems and convict the innocent.

## The fix: grade, weight, then divide

The improvement is mechanical, not clever: **not all inquiries are worth the same, so stop counting them the same.**

- **Purchase-ready inquiries** — volume prices, sample requests, spec discussions: one step from negotiation. Full weight
- **Ordinary inquiries** — genuine questions, product details: real but earlier. Partial weight
- **Shallow actions** — favorites, add-to-carts, contact taps: intent at its thinnest. Tiny weight or none

Divide spend by the weighted total instead of the raw count, and the result moves when quality moves — which is the entire point. What weight values to use has no universal answer; derive them from your own funnel (which inquiry grade most often becomes an order, gets the most weight). What matters is fixing the weights and never changing them mid-stream — comparability is the asset.

## The benchmark: your history, not the industry's

With the cost measured properly, the next question is the target. The commonest mistake is searching for an "industry average inquiry cost" to compare against. Categories, order values, and inquiry quality vary so much that the average describes no one — including you.

Set the line from **your own record**:

1. Take your normal delivery months — promotions and holidays excluded
2. Rank their inquiry costs; the band they form is your normal operating range
3. That band is the pass line: meaningfully above it is a problem, meaningfully below it deserves budget

A benchmark built from your history makes every month's movement readable. It is the same principle as audience judgment — self-comparison only.

## FAQ

### What is the right way to calculate B2B ad inquiry cost?

Don't divide spend by the raw inquiry count. Grade inquiries by quality (purchase-ready, casual, shallow leads), weight them, and divide spend by the weighted total — the result reflects what the ad actually buys.

### Where should the inquiry-cost target come from?

Your own history. Take your normal delivery months, rank their inquiry costs, and set the baseline from that band — not from industry averages.

### Why not benchmark against industry average inquiry costs?

Category, order value, and inquiry quality differ too much for one number to mean anything across stores. The same cost figure can be excellent for one store and disastrous for another.
