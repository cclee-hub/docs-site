## TL;DR

"Which product do we promote next?" has a more reliable answer than trend-guessing: the products already selling on organic traffic. Screen them through three gates — buyer count, revenue scale, median-relative conversion — and promote only what passes all three.

## You promoted a hunch, and the hunch burned the budget

Every cycle brings the same question: what do we promote next?

The common selection process is vibes: a hot category, a nice-looking listing, the owner's hunch. Two months of spend later, the lesson repeats itself: **a product that cannot sell on organic traffic does not become sellable because it has an ad budget.**

Paid traffic is an amplifier, not a life raft. It scales demand that already exists. The most reliable place to find scalable demand is your own store's organic order flow.

## The three gates

Gate the candidate on what it does **without ads**:

### Gate one: enough distinct buying customers

Organic buyers over the window must clear a base — not one or two lucky orders, but a batch of different companies buying. Buyer count measures the breadth of demand: a crowd buying means amplification has something to amplify.

### Gate two: revenue with scale

Buyers alone aren't enough; the order value must add up. Thin, scattered transactions mean demand exists but is too shallow to carry ad costs. This gate filters out the "people buy it, but it'll never pay for traffic" tail.

### Gate three: conversion no worse than the store median

The decisive one: **the candidate's organic conversion rate must not sit below the middle of your currently promoted products.**

Practically: rank your promoted products by conversion rate and take the one in the middle — that number is your store's demonstrated ability to digest traffic today. A candidate below it converts worse than what you already run; the same traffic monetizes worse in its hands. Promoting it doesn't add revenue, it dilutes the account.

## Using the gates

| Result | Call |
|--------|------|
| All three pass | Promotion candidate — enters a funded test |
| Any gate missed | Wait; keep accumulating organic data |
| Conversion clearly below the median | Don't promote — fix the product first |

The last row is the classic error: conversion fails, so "push more traffic at it." Conversion is a product problem; ads scale the problem along with everything else — more volume, more efficiently wasted.

## Make it a loop

Screening isn't a one-time audit; it's a monthly rotation: **read organic data → apply the gates → test the passers → promote the winners → process the losers through keep-or-stop rules.** Rotate the stock, and the account always holds validated newcomers instead of hunches.

<InfoBox variant="warning" title="One sentence to remember">

A promotion candidate = buyer count with breadth + order value with scale + conversion at or above the store median. Three for three, or nothing — ads amplify working products; they don't rescue broken ones.

</InfoBox>

## FAQ

### How do I pick which unadvertised products to promote?

Gate them on organic performance: enough distinct buying customers, meaningful order value, and a conversion rate no worse than your store's promoted-product median. All three pass, test it; any miss, wait.

### Why are organically selling products the best ad candidates?

The market has already validated the product; ads only amplify. Promoting an unvalidated product pays to discover failure — promoting a selling one pays to scale success.

### What does 'no worse than the store median' mean in practice?

Rank your currently promoted products by conversion rate and take the middle one as the reference. A candidate below that median converts worse than what you already run — it would drag the account.
