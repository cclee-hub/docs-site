## TL;DR

"It has orders" and "the ads work" are different claims. The ads' ledger only records buyers who came through the ad. When attributed revenue doesn't cover spend — while the product sells anyway — the ads are a toll booth on organic traffic. Pause-worthy, with one attribution-health check first.

## The product with twenty thousand in orders and a failing ad

You recommend pausing a product's ads. Pushback: "It did twenty thousand in orders this month. Why stop?"

Unbundle the ledger: total revenue, yes — twenty thousand. But **revenue attributed to the ads: a few hundred, against thousands in ad spend.** The buyers who came through the ad didn't cover its own bill. The rest of that twenty thousand was coming anyway — brand searches, repeat buyers, marketplace organic.

The ads' actual role: collecting a fee on traffic that never needed them.

## Two ledgers, two questions

| Ledger | Question it answers | Decides |
|--------|--------------------|---------|
| Total store revenue | Is this product selling well? | Product decisions |
| Attributed revenue | Is the ad bringing buyers? | Ad keep-or-stop |

Ad analysis runs only on the second ledger. When **attributed revenue ÷ ad spend** sits well under 1 — the ad brings in less money than it costs — the placement loses money on its own terms, and the product's popularity doesn't change the arithmetic.

Confusing the ledgers awards the ad a free ride on the product's merits: keep the "successful" campaign alive on orders it never brought.

## One check before pulling the plug: is the attribution healthy?

Low attributed share has two opposite causes, and they want opposite actions:

- **True failure**: inquiries shrinking along with attribution — the ads genuinely stopped bringing buyers. Pause
- **Recording gap**: inquiries healthy, attribution oddly low — possibly an attribution miss, punishing a working ad for a bookkeeping artifact. Audit manually first

And one mirror-image red flag: spend is real but inquiries and leads sit at single digits while attributed revenue looks high — a "high attribution" on almost no signal is a data artifact, not a performance. Distrust it in both directions.

## The floor: some failures no order can offset

Finally, a boundary that sales volume cannot cross. Some ad failures are **acquisition-path failures**: placements that barely spend or click at all, delivery that burns budget and produces zero inquiries. These don't get rescued by the product's orders — the ad's own function is already dead. Revenue-based defenses apply to ambiguous, data-thin cases; they don't revive a path that never connected.

<InfoBox variant="warning" title="One sentence to remember">

Ads live or die on their own ledger: did attributed revenue cover spend, and are inquiries healthy? The product's popularity is the product's achievement — don't let the ads claim it, and don't let a recording gap frame it.

</InfoBox>

## FAQ

### A product is clearly selling — why would ads for it deserve a pause?

Ads are judged on their own ledger: if buyers who came through the ad (attributed revenue) don't cover the ad spend, the orders are mostly organic and the ads are paying a toll on sales that would happen anyway.

### How do I tell whether orders actually came from the ads?

Compare attributed revenue against total store revenue for the product, against spend. Heavy spend with a tiny attributed share means the ads aren't bringing customers — the store's own traffic is.

### Does low attribution share always mean the ads failed?

No — first check inquiries. Healthy inquiry volume with low attribution may be a recording gap and needs a manual audit; shrinking inquiries with low attribution is true failure.
