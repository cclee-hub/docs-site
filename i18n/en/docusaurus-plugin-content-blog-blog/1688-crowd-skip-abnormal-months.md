## TL;DR

Chinese New Year, Black Friday, a change of hero product — these months move every audience in your store at once. When the whole world shifts, single-audience "deterioration" is a mirage. The discipline is to freeze: no bid decisions in abnormal months, and the month gets skipped in trends.

## You re-priced the audience; the calendar re-priced everything

A big sale ends. The audience report is a wall of red: inquiry costs up everywhere. You cut the worst movers and rewarded the ones that "held up."

A month later, numbers normalize — and you discover the audiences you rewarded were merely the ones the promotion didn't reach, while the ones you cut were the steadiest performers of the whole quarter.

Sale-month data is not wrong. It is answering a different question: "how does this audience behave when the world is on fire?" — which is not the question bid management needs answered.

## Detecting an abnormal month: one store-wide number

You don't need a calendar. You need one metric: **store-wide blended inquiry cost, month over month** (total spend ÷ total inquiries across all audiences).

In normal operation, that number moves within a band — high teens percent at most. When it approaches **doubling**, something structural happened: traffic mix, buying rhythm, product structure, or a platform policy shift. At that point every per-audience reading has lost its reference frame. A bent ruler measures nothing.

## The months to watch

| Scenario | Why the data breaks |
|----------|--------------------|
| Major holidays (New Year, and market-wide shutdowns) | Procurement halts industry-wide; inquiry structure deforms |
| Mega sale seasons (platform-wide festivals) | Traffic mix shifts violently; usually-quiet audiences spike |
| Hero-product switch | Store audience composition migrates; history loses comparability |
| Major platform rule changes | Allocation logic changes; before and after are different worlds |

The common thread: not "an audience changed" but "the world changed." No per-audience adjustment is meaningful inside that.

## What to actually do

Two moves, that's all:

1. **No bid decisions this month.** Read the reports, log the numbers, but raise/hold/lower decisions wait.
2. **Skip the month in trends.** Trend logic needs consecutive comparable months; an abnormal month sits in the sequence like a spring — it stores distortion and releases it into your next decision. Compare normal months to normal months only.

This is not passivity about promotions. Sale seasons deserve their own playbook — warm-up pricing, event audiences, deliberate aggression. The freeze rule governs exactly one thing: **never let abnormal-month data flow into routine bid decisions.**

<InfoBox variant="warning" title="One sentence to remember">

When the store-wide blended inquiry cost nearly doubles month over month, freeze: no bid changes that month, and the month is skipped in every trend. Let holidays be holidays — judgment only reads normal months.

</InfoBox>

## FAQ

### Can I use sale-season data to adjust B2B ad bids?

No. In promotion and holiday months the whole store's inquiry costs jump together, so per-audience moves are mostly false signals. Freeze bid decisions for the month.

### How do I detect an abnormal month?

Watch the store-wide blended inquiry cost month over month. Normal months move within roughly 20%; a move near doubling is structural — treat the month as abnormal.

### What should I do after an abnormal month passes?

Skip it. When trends need three comparable months, use only normal completed months — never let a holiday month sit inside a trend line.
