## TL;DR

A paused campaign is neither dead nor free to revive — every restart re-pays the learning cost. Treat each paused campaign as a file to be evaluated: was the data deep enough, was it profitable before pausing, did conversion meet the store's bar, and can the attribution be trusted?

## The restart that replayed the loss

Scaling season: many campaigns created, a batch later paused. When targets rise again, you bulk-restart the paused list. A month later, the ones that lost money before lose money again.

Restarting is not a free retry. **Each revival burns the learning period again** — so "restart or not" deserves a real evaluation, not "it ran before, run it again."

## Four questions before the switch

### One: was the data deep enough to ever judge it?

Before pausing, did it have real clicks and one to two settlement periods of delivery? A campaign that never accumulated evaluative data wasn't judged — it was abandoned. Restarting is fine, but only with a plan to fund and persist long enough for a verdict this time; otherwise you're scheduling the same abandonment twice.

### Two: was it profitable at pause time?

Read the pre-pause attributed account: did buyers who came through the ad generate enough to cover its spend? If attributed revenue didn't cover cost, the acquisition path itself failed. Restarting with the same targeting, pricing, and creative replays the same movie. No restart — unless something real changes: different audience, different bid structure, different creative.

### Three: did conversion meet the store's bar?

Three gates: **enough distinct buying customers** (not one or two lucky orders), **meaningful attributed revenue**, **conversion rate no worse than the store's promoted-product norm**. All three passing means the path was validated and the pause was probably just a budget shuffle — these are the first candidates for revival.

### Four: can the attribution be trusted?

Two red flags in the pre-pause numbers: **attributed revenue far exceeding the product's total store revenue** (the ad out-sold the whole store?), or **solid spend with single-digit inquiries and leads**. Either shape means the ledger itself is suspect — and "worth restarting," built on suspect data, is suspect. Audit manually before deciding.

Plus one timing rule: a campaign paused less than a settlement cycle ago hasn't finished its tail — orders still post back (the mechanics in [Why marketplace ad data needs 16 days before you judge it](/blog/1688-p4p-ad-data-16-day-settlement)). Let the ledger close, then evaluate.

## The table

| Pre-pause state | Restart call |
|-----------------|--------------|
| Data too thin (few clicks, short run) | Only with a real plan to fund a verdict this time |
| Attributed revenue never covered spend | No — unless the setup changes |
| All three conversion gates passed | Restart first |
| Attribution far above store total / single-digit inquiries | Audit data trust first |
| Paused within one settlement cycle | Wait for the ledger to close |

## FAQ

### Can a paused B2B marketplace campaign be restarted?

Yes, but evaluate first: were its pre-pause buyers, attributed transactions, and conversion rate up to standard? Data that passed is worth restarting; never flip the switch on a feeling.

### Should I restart a campaign that was losing money when paused?

No. If attributed transactions didn't cover ad spend before the pause, the acquisition path itself failed — a restart with the same setup repeats the result.

### When can I evaluate a campaign I just paused?

After settlement. For a week or two post-pause, transactions keep posting back; the true pre-pause picture only exists once the ledger closes.
